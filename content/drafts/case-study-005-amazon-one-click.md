**[EN] Intro**

Online shopping in the late 1990s meant typing your address and card number into form after form, on every site, for every order. Each form was a place to give up. Amazon's founding insight about checkout was almost rude in its simplicity: the store should remember. Everything else in this case study — the patent, the button, the membership — is that one idea, defended and extended for decades.

**[EN] 1. The defaults do the work**

Amazon saves your address, your payment method and your delivery preferences the first time you use them. From then on, checkout is mostly a review, not a form. This looks like a small convenience. It is actually the whole machine: every field the store remembers is a field that cannot be typed wrong, cannot make you stop, and cannot give you a minute to change your mind.

**Principle Card — Defaults and Memory**
Rules applied: Default Effect · Recognition Rather Than Recall
People stick with what is already set. A store that pre-fills your life removes recall, typing and doubt in one move. The behaviour underneath is trust through familiarity: the details feel right because they are yours, shown back to you.

**[EN] 2. One click, patented**

In 1999 the US patent office granted Amazon a patent on ordering with a single click: see the item, click once, the stored address and card do the rest. Competitors had to license it or add steps Amazon did not have. The patent expired in 2017, but by then the behaviour it created had long become the expectation. Notice what the click really removes: not effort, but the pause. Between wanting and owning, there is almost no time left to reconsider.

**Principle Card — Friction Removal**
Rules applied: Friction · Fitts's Law
Every step between intention and action leaks people. One big, obvious action with no form after it leaks almost nobody. The behaviour underneath is momentum: a desire that meets no resistance becomes a purchase before doubt has time to arrive.

**[EN] 3. Two doors: the cart and Buy Now**

Amazon never forced everyone through one path. Add to Cart suits the shopper who is still gathering and comparing. Buy Now skips the basket entirely and takes a single item straight to a one-screen confirmation. Two buttons, two states of mind. The design respects that the same person is a browser in the morning and a buyer with one urgent need at night.

**Principle Card — Match the Path to the Intent**
Rules applied: Hick's Law · Progressive Disclosure
People freeze when one flow tries to serve every intention at once. Splitting the journey by intent cuts the decisions on each path: collectors get room to collect, buyers get a straight line. The behaviour underneath is simple: the closer the next step matches what I already decided, the more likely I am to take it.

**[EN] 4. Prime pays the painful part in advance**

The last big friction in online shopping was never the form. It was the delivery fee at the end, and the wait after it. Prime moved both: one yearly payment, then delivery that feels free and fast on every order. Once the fee is prepaid, it stops appearing in your decision at all. Members start at Amazon not because every price is lowest, but because the painful part of buying was paid for long ago.

**Principle Card — Pain of Paying, Decoupled**
Rules applied: Pain of Paying · Sunk Cost
People feel a purchase most when paying and consuming happen at the same moment. Prime separates them by months. The yearly fee fades into the past, each order feels light, and the membership itself becomes a reason to use what you already paid for.

**[MY] 4. Prime က နာကျင်တဲ့ အပိုင်းကို ကြို ပေးတယ်**

**[EN] 5. Fast, but with an undo**

One click would be reckless without a way back. Amazon surrounds the speed with safety: a window to cancel or change the order, confirmation emails, order tracking, and returns that are famously easy to start. The safety is not a footnote to the speed. It is what makes the speed acceptable. People dare to buy in one click precisely because un-buying feels possible.

**Principle Card — Risk Reversal**
Rules applied: Risk Reversal · Trust
People act faster when the cost of being wrong is low. An obvious escape route does not slow a good checkout down; it is the reason people stop reading the fine print and press the button. Speed and safety are not enemies. Safety is the licence for speed.

**[MY] 5. မြန်တယ်။ ဒါပေမဲ့ ပြန် ဖျက်လို့ ရတယ်**

ပြန်လမ်း မရှိဘဲ တစ်ချက် နှိပ်တာက မဆင်ခြင်ဘဲ လုပ်တာ ဖြစ်မယ်။ Amazon က အမြန်နှုန်းကို ဘေးကင်းမှုနဲ့ ပတ်လည်ထားတယ်။ Order ကို ပယ်ဖျက်ဖို့ ဒါမှမဟုတ် ပြောင်းဖို့ အချိန် အခန်း တစ်ခု၊ အတည်ပြု email တွေ၊ order ခြေရာခံတာ၊ စရလွယ်တာ နာမည်ကြီးတဲ့ ပြန်ပို့မှုတွေ။ ဘေးကင်းမှုက အမြန်နှုန်းရဲ့ အောက်ခြေ မှတ်ချက် မဟုတ်ဘူး။ အမြန်နှုန်းကို လက်ခံလို့ ရအောင် လုပ်တာပဲ။ လူတွေက တစ်ချက် နှိပ်ပြီး ဝယ်ရဲတယ်။ ပြန် ဖျက်လို့ ရတယ် ခံစားရလို့ပဲ တိတိကျကျ။

**Principle Card — Risk Reversal**
Applied လုပ်တဲ့ rules: Risk Reversal · Trust
မှားတဲ့ အခ နည်းရင် လူက ပို မြန်မြန် လုပ်တယ်။ ရှင်းလင်းတဲ့ ထွက်ပေါက် က checkout ကောင်းကို မနှေးစေဘူး။ လူတွေ စာသေး မဖတ်တော့ဘဲ button နှိပ်တဲ့ အကြောင်းက အဲဒါပဲ။ အမြန်နှုန်းနဲ့ ဘေးကင်းမှုက ရန်သူ မဟုတ်ဘူး။ ဘေးကင်းမှုက အမြန်နှုန်းရဲ့ လိုင်စင်ပဲ။

**[EN] The honest summary**

Removing friction also removes the pause where a person reconsiders. That pause was costing Amazon sales, but it was also protecting shoppers from impulse buys, wrong sizes and orders placed by a child with a phone. One-click design is not automatically good design; it is powerful design, and power needs the guardrails this case study ended with: clear totals, a cancel window, painless returns. Copy the memory and the defaults. Copy the undo just as carefully. A checkout that is fast and forgiving respects the buyer. A checkout that is only fast respects the seller.

**[MY] အမှန်အတိုင်း အကျဉ်းချုပ်**

တိုက်မိမှု ဖယ်တာက လူ ပြန် စဉ်းစားတဲ့ ခဏ ရပ်ချိန်ကိုပါ ဖယ်ပစ်တယ်။ အဲဒီ ရပ်ချိန်က Amazon ကို အရောင်း ဆုံးရှုံးစေခဲ့တယ်။ ဒါပေမဲ့ ဈေးဝယ်သူတွေကို စိတ်လိုက် လက်ရ ဝယ်တာ၊ အရွယ် မှားတာ နဲ့ ဖုန်း ကိုင်ထားတဲ့ ကလေး တစ်ယောက် တင်တဲ့ order တွေကနေလည်း ကာကွယ်ခဲ့တယ်။ One-click design က အလိုအလျောက် ကောင်းတဲ့ design မဟုတ်ဘူး။ အင်အားကြီးတဲ့ design ပဲ။ အင်အားက ဒီ case study အဆုံးမှာ ပါတဲ့ အကာအရံတွေ လိုတယ်။ ရှင်းလင်းတဲ့ စုစုပေါင်းတွေ၊ ပယ်ဖျက်တဲ့ အချိန် အခန်း တစ်ခု၊ မနာကျင်တဲ့ ပြန်ပို့မှုတွေ။ မှတ်ဉာဏ်နဲ့ default တွေကို ကူးပါ။ Undo ကိုပါ အဲဒီလိုပဲ သေချာ ကူးပါ။ မြန်ပြီး ခွင့်လွှတ်တဲ့ checkout က ဝယ်သူကို လေးစားတယ်။ မြန်ရုံပဲ မြန်တဲ့ checkout က ရောင်းသူကိုပဲ လေးစားတယ်။
