**[EN] Title: Show the Work, and the Wait Feels Worth It**

**[EN] The spinner that tells you nothing**

You search for a flight. The screen goes blank except for a small spinning circle. Ten seconds pass. Twenty. The circle says nothing about where your answer is, who is being asked, or whether anything is happening at all. The wait feels long, and slightly insulting, like being left on hold with no music.

Now picture the other version. The same search, but the screen talks to you. Searching this airline. Now this one. Checking 200 fares. Found a cheaper one, holding it. Same wait. Maybe even a longer wait. And yet it feels shorter, and the results feel better when they arrive.

**[MY] ဘာမှ မပြောတဲ့ spinner**

လေယာဉ် လက်မှတ် ရှာတယ် ဆိုပါစို့။ Screen ပေါ်မှာ လည်နေတဲ့ စက်ဝိုင်း သေးသေးလေး တစ်ခုပဲ ကျန်တယ်။ စက္ကန့် ဆယ် ကျော်တယ်။ နှစ်ဆယ် ကျော်တယ်။ စက်ဝိုင်းက ဘာမှ မပြောဘူး။ မင်းရဲ့ အဖြေ ဘယ်ရောက်နေလဲ။ ဘယ်သူ့ကို မေးနေလဲ။ ဘာမှ ဖြစ်နေတာမှ ဟုတ်ရဲ့လား။ ဘာမှ မသိရဘူး။ စောင့်ရတာ ရှည်တယ်လို့ ခံစားရတယ်။ နည်းနည်း စော်ကားခံရသလိုလည်း ဖြစ်တယ်။ တေးသွားတောင် မပါဘဲ hold ထားခံရသလိုပဲ။

**[EN] The travel site experiment**

Ryan Buell and Michael Norton studied exactly this with a travel search website. One version returned results the normal way. The other version practiced what they called operational transparency: it showed the work happening behind the screen, which airlines were being searched, and a progress display that let users watch the effort unfold.

The result surprised people who think speed is everything. Users valued the results from the transparent version more. Showing the work made the service feel worth more, even when the wait was longer. The effort the company was spending on the user turned out to be something users could feel, and they repaid it with trust and patience.

**[EN] The rule**

People judge a wait by what they can see, not only by how long it takes. Work that is visible feels valuable. Work that is hidden feels like nothing is happening, and nothing happening feels like being ignored.

A silent process and a busy process with the same duration are not the same experience. One is a company working for you. The other is a wall.

**[MY] စည်းမျဉ်းက ဒီလိုပါ**

လူတွေက စောင့်ရတာကို ကြာချိန် တစ်ခုတည်း နဲ့ မဆုံးဖြတ်ဘူး။ မြင်ရတာနဲ့လည်း ဆုံးဖြတ်တယ်။ မြင်ရတဲ့ အလုပ်က တန်ဖိုး ရှိတယ်လို့ ခံစားရတယ်။ ဝှက်ထားတဲ့ အလုပ်က ဘာမှ မဖြစ်နေသလို ခံစားရတယ်။ ဘာမှ မဖြစ်နေဘူးဆိုတာ လျစ်လျူရှု ခံရသလို ခံစားရတယ်။

**[EN] The pizza tracker everyone copied**

Domino's built the famous product version of this. The Pizza Tracker does not make the pizza cook faster. It simply reports the stages. Your order is being prepped. It is in the oven. Quality check. Out for delivery, with the driver's name attached. Customers loved it, because the wait stopped being a mystery and became a story with their pizza inside it.

Software has quiet versions of the same trick everywhere. "We're checking 200 hotels" under a search bar. An import tool that names each file as it processes it. A status line that says what is happening now, instead of a bar crawling toward a number nobody believes. Each one turns your system's invisible labour into the user's evidence that somebody is on the case.

**[EN] What this means for your product**

Anywhere your product makes people wait, you are choosing between a wall and a window. Uploads, exports, searches, AI generation, payment checks, sync. The wait itself is often fixed by engineering you cannot change this quarter. The visibility of the work is design, and design can ship this week.

Be careful about the honest version of this, though. Showing real steps from your real process is operational transparency. Inventing fake steps to look busy is theatre, and users who catch it once will never trust your progress bars again.

**[MY] မင်း product အတွက် ဘာအဓိပ္ပာယ်လဲ**

မင်း product က လူတွေကို စောင့်ခိုင်းတဲ့ နေရာတိုင်းမှာ မင်းက နံရံနဲ့ ပြတင်းပေါက် ကြားမှာ ရွေးနေတာ။ Upload, export, search, AI generation, payment check, sync အကုန် အဲဒီလိုပဲ။ စောင့်ရတဲ့ အချိန် ကိုယ်တိုင်က ဒီ quarter မှာ မပြောင်းနိုင်တဲ့ engineering နဲ့ ကပ်နေတတ်တယ်။ အလုပ်လုပ်နေတာ မြင်ရအောင် လုပ်တာကတော့ design ပဲ။ Design က ဒီ အပတ်ထဲမှာ ship လို့ရတယ်။

**[EN] Working rules**

- Replace naked spinners with narrated waits. "Searching 200 hotels, 140 checked" beats a circle, even if the circle is honestly faster.
- Name the work in the user's language of value. "Comparing live prices" means something. "Processing request" means nothing. One tells them effort is being spent on them. The other tells them a machine is busy.
- Show steps you actually perform, in the order you perform them. If the user catches the display lying, every future wait in your product gets more expensive.
- Let the last step land visibly. A clear "done, here is your result" closes the story the progress display started. A wait that fades silently into results wastes the trust it just built.
