**[EN] Intro**

A video store had one poster per film. Netflix has many, and it picks which one you see. That single fact explains the whole homepage: there is no Netflix catalogue, there are millions of personal ones, rebuilt for each viewer from what they watched, paused, skipped and loved. The design question Netflix answers every night is not "what do we have?" It is "what will this one person press play on in the next thirty seconds?"

**[EN] 1. Same film, different face**

Netflix documented this openly on its technology blog: the artwork for one title changes with your taste. If your history leans toward comedies, a drama's thumbnail may lead with its funny actor. If you love a certain star, their face carries the image. The film did not change. The invitation did. Each version is honest about one true side of the title, and personal about which side you get shown first.

**Principle Card — Personalized Relevance**
Rules applied: Personalization · Relevance
People click what looks like it was made for them. A thumbnail is a promise, and a promise in your own taste language gets believed faster. The behaviour underneath is recognition: the face, genre or mood you already love acts as a shortcut for a decision you have not made yet.

**[EN] 2. Continue Watching: the unfinished pulls you back**

Near the top of the homepage, your half-finished episodes wait in a row, each with a thin red progress bar. One tap and you are back mid-scene, exactly where you left. Netflix did not invent the pull of an unfinished thing. It just gave it a permanent seat at the front of the store, ahead of everything new it wants to sell you.

**Principle Card — The Unfinished Task**
Rules applied: Zeigarnik Effect · Recognition Rather Than Recall
People remember and return to tasks they started but did not finish. A progress bar makes the unfinished state visible, and resuming beats choosing: no search, no decision, just continue. The behaviour underneath is closure-seeking. An open loop nags until it closes.

**[EN] 3. Top 10: the crowd decides for you**

One row on the homepage simply lists what everyone in your country is watching, with giant numbers from one to ten. It answers the question tired people actually ask at night: not "what is best?" but "what is everyone else watching?" When your own taste feels exhausted, the crowd lends you theirs.

**Principle Card — Social Proof as a Shelf**
Rules applied: Social Proof · Hick's Law
People copy the choices of people around them, especially when deciding feels costly. A ranked list of ten also shrinks the decision itself: ten options, pre-filtered by millions of viewers, is a much smaller problem than an endless catalogue. The behaviour underneath is borrowed confidence.

**[MY] 3. Top 10: လူအုပ်က မင်းအတွက် ဆုံးဖြတ်ပေးတယ်**

ပင်မ စာမျက်နှာပေါ်က အတန်း တစ်ခုက မင်းရဲ့ နိုင်ငံမှာ လူတိုင်း ဘာ ကြည့်နေလဲ စာရင်း ပြတယ်။ နံပါတ် ကြီးကြီးတွေနဲ့ တစ်ကနေ ဆယ်အထိ။ သူက ပင်ပန်းတဲ့ လူတွေ ညမှာ တကယ် မေးတဲ့ မေးခွန်းကို ဖြေတယ်။ "အကောင်းဆုံး ဘာလဲ" မဟုတ်ဘူး။ "တခြား လူတိုင်း ဘာ ကြည့်နေလဲ" ပဲ။ မင်းရဲ့ ကိုယ်ပိုင် အကြိုက်က ကုန်သလို ခံစားရရင် လူအုပ်က သူတို့ဟာကို မင်းကို ငှားတယ်။

**Principle Card — Social Proof as a Shelf**
Applied လုပ်တဲ့ rules: Social Proof · Hick's Law

**[EN] 4. The next episode just starts**

An episode ends, and instead of a decision, there is a countdown. Do nothing, and the next episode begins. Doing nothing is the strongest move in the whole interface, because it is not a move at all. One episode becomes an evening through a series of moments where continuing was easier than stopping. Netflix heard the criticism of this design for years, and eventually gave viewers a setting to turn autoplay off — an admission that the default is a decision the company was making for you.

**Principle Card — The Default Decides**
Rules applied: Default Effect · Friction
People accept the path that unfolds without action. Every autoplay removes one exit point from the evening, and exit points are where viewing sessions naturally end. The behaviour underneath is inertia: a story in motion stays in motion, and the remote stays on the table.

**[MY] 4. နောက် အပိုင်းက ဒီအတိုင်း စသွားတယ်**

အပိုင်း တစ်ခု ပြီးတယ်။ ဆုံးဖြတ်ချက် အစား countdown တစ်ခု ရှိတယ်။ ဘာမှ မလုပ်ဘူး။ နောက် အပိုင်းက စတယ်။ ဘာမှ မလုပ်တာက interface တစ်ခုလုံးမှာ အင်အား အကြီးဆုံး ရွေ့လျားမှုပဲ။ အဲဒါက ရွေ့လျားမှုကို မဟုတ်လို့ပဲ။ အပိုင်း တစ်ခုက ဆက်တာက ရပ်တာထက် ပို လွယ်တဲ့ အခိုက်အတန့်တွေ အတွဲလိုက်နဲ့ ညနေ တစ်ခု ဖြစ်သွားတယ်။ Netflix က ဒီ design အပေါ် ဝေဖန်မှုကို နှစ်နဲ့ချီ ကြားခဲ့တယ်။ နောက်ဆုံး ကြည့်သူတွေကို autoplay ပိတ်ဖို့ setting တစ်ခု ပေးခဲ့တယ်။ Default က ကုမ္ပဏီက မင်းအတွက် လုပ်နေတဲ့ ဆုံးဖြတ်ချက် ဖြစ်တယ် ဆိုတာ ဝန်ခံချက်ပဲ။

**[EN] 5. Your homepage is an experiment that never ends**

Rows move. Artwork changes. Two neighbours with the same account type can open Netflix and see meaningfully different shelves. Netflix tests variations on real audiences constantly and keeps what earns more viewing. This means the homepage you designed an opinion about last year is not quite the homepage that exists now. It also means every click you make tonight is quietly voting on what your screen looks like tomorrow.

**Principle Card — The Measurement Loop**
Rules applied: A/B Testing · Feedback Loop
People reveal their taste by behaviour, not by surveys. A system that measures every hover and abandon can tune itself faster than any design team's intuition. The behaviour underneath is the click as a ballot: small, honest, and counted millions of times a night.

**[EN] The honest summary**

Personalization at Netflix is genuinely helpful and quietly narrowing at the same time. The artwork that speaks your language can also bait a click the film itself does not repay. Autoplay serves the metric of hours watched, which is Netflix's evening goal, not always yours. To its credit, the company publishes its methods, and it did add controls when viewers pushed back on autoplay. The lesson for designers is not "personalize everything". It is that every default is a decision made on the user's behalf. Make it, measure it, but hand the user the switch — and notice how rarely they forgive a product that will not give it back.

**[MY] အမှန်အတိုင်း အကျဉ်းချုပ်**

Netflix မှာ personalization က တကယ် အထောက်အကူ ဖြစ်တယ်။ တစ်ချိန်တည်းမှာ တိတ်တိတ် ကျဉ်းလည်း ကျဉ်းစေတယ်။ မင်းရဲ့ ဘာသာစကားနဲ့ ပြောတဲ့ artwork က ရုပ်ရှင် ကိုယ်တိုင်က ပြန် မဆပ်တဲ့ နှိပ်ချက်ကို မြှူလည်း မြှူနိုင်တယ်။ Autoplay က ကြည့်တဲ့ နာရီ ဆိုတဲ့ တိုင်းတာမှုကို ထမ်းတယ်။ အဲဒါက Netflix ရဲ့ ညနေ ပန်းတိုင်ပဲ။ မင်းဟာ အမြဲ မဟုတ်ဘူး။ ကုမ္ပဏီက သူ့ရဲ့ နည်းလမ်းတွေကို ထုတ်ပြန်တယ်။ ကြည့်သူတွေ autoplay အပေါ် ပြန် တွန်းတော့ ထိန်းချုပ်မှုတွေ ထည့်ပေးခဲ့တယ်။ အဲဒါ သူ့ အတွက် ဂုဏ်ယူစရာပဲ။ Designer တွေအတွက် သင်ခန်းစာက "အကုန်လုံးကို personalize လုပ်ပါ" မဟုတ်ဘူး။ Default တိုင်းက user ကိုယ်စား လုပ်တဲ့ ဆုံးဖြတ်ချက် ဖြစ်တယ် ဆိုတာပဲ။ လုပ်ပါ၊ တိုင်းပါ၊ ဒါပေမဲ့ switch ကို user လက်ထဲ ပေးပါ။ ပြန်မပေးတဲ့ product ကို လူတွေ ခွင့်လွှတ်တာ ဘယ်လောက် ရှားလဲ သတိထားပါ။
